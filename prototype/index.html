How a run goes: chapters of short encounters — archers (steady volleys), cavalry (the charge speeds up each spell), and a boss (its incantations follow a rhythm pattern, off-beat glyphs included). Clear a chapter and the incantations grow: short two-word spells → longer derived words → three-word invocations → numbers → symbols. Spells are phrases in one invented tongue (the gloss under the word says what you're casting), and each encounter keeps to one school of words, so they repeat and become learnable.
  • Riffs (Glyphs setting, the default): glyphs come in key patterns played in quick 8th/16th bursts with a breath between. Every spell is a sigil: its strokes share one shape, and the shape is the rune a clean spell earns — pillar (one key repeated) earth, wave (two fingers trading) water, sweep (a roll across neighbouring fingers) air, burst (mirrored pairs hand to hand, opening from the index fingers) fire. The thread through its glyphs glows in the rune's color. From chapter 2 a sigil ends in a word, its inscription. Each encounter repeats a handful of strokes, so learn them as one movement. The chapters open the keyboard row by row, starting with the home row. "words" brings back the runic-tongue spells.
  • Penalty: switch to strict mid-run — one wrong key kills the whole incantation. Is this motivating or rage-quit for a learner?
  • Pacing: are encounters short enough early on, and does each chapter feel like a step up? "start at" jumps straight to a later chapter.
  • Runes are how you fight: typing no longer attacks. Every clean spell (no glyph missed) earns a rune of its element (the lane most of its glyphs ride: fire, water, earth, air); defeating an enemy earns a mend rune (bosses 2). Press Space — time stops (music and timers too) and the letters hide, but only for a few seconds (6 s in chapter 1, 3.5 s by chapter 6: the bar around the ring; empty, the cast fizzles). The faster you finish a name, the harder it hits: ×1.5 instantly down to ×0.5 at the buzzer (bolts and mend) — the panel opens into the spellbook; type a name to cast: vorkai fire, gamkai water, brykai earth, wynkai air (bolts; ×2 against the enemy's weakness, shown on its health bar), pyrhal mend (+30 HP), dunfen ward (always ready). A wrong letter fizzles (charge kept) — fizzle twice in a row and it backfires for 12 damage (a successful cast resets it); Space again cancels; the ring closes itself when a glyph reaches the line.
  • Charged attacks: every enemy charges a big attack — the ring over its head fills; in the last bar it counts down the beats. Cast dunfen then: the ward holds for 5 seconds and counters the charged attack back at the enemy. Missed glyphs still let the enemy hit you. The countdown is loud on purpose: a rising doom pulse each beat, the hit line turns red, the enemy's screen edge flashes.
  • Empowered: you charge too — the ring over the caster fills over 20 beats, like the enemy's. Full, it rings a bright chime and opens 5 seconds (cyan aura, cyan screen edge) in which every bolt rune hits ×2.
  • Flawless spells (every glyph PERFECT) trigger the incantation's school power at once, no cast needed: storm Gale (glyphs and the charge pushed back a bar), stone Bulwark (next strike blocked), shadow Veil (next 6 glyphs take any key of the right finger), tide Stillwater (next 2 spells 20% slower), flame Kindle (next bolt ×2).
  • Lanes: can you read the finger from color+position before you type it? The element + finger labels are under the hit line.
  • When to press: the letter crossing the white line — the tile filling its outlined slot. The two thin lines around the hit line are the PERFECT window (±55 ms); the tile touching the line at all is the GOOD window (±140 ms). Every judgment shows its error in ms, and the bar at the top of the battle stage plots your last 20 presses.
  • Calibration: if the click doesn't land when the letter crosses the line, slide audio. If your presses sit consistently early/late on the error bar, press "set input from last 20 hits" (or slide input). Both are saved in this browser.
